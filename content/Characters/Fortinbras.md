Prince of Norway. Decisive, ambitious, and disciplined. He channels his military energy into action (even for a worthless piece of land), a foil to Hamlet's hesitation to act.

### Psychological Arc
Fortinbras honestly has no internal psychological arc in the play. That is kind of the point. He starts and ends the play as the same single-minded, disciplined man of action. Initially he is driven by pride and vengeance to reclaim the lands his father lost to Old King Hamlet, but after negotiation he redirects his military aggression toward Poland. He likely did this because of diplomatic pressure, which demonstrates his pragmatism and adaptability as opposed to obsessive brooding.

### Key Relationships
- **[[Hamlet]]:** His structural [[foil]]. Fortinbras acts without questioning anything, much less meaning. Hamlet, on the other hand, questions everything and as a result struggles to act. Ironically, Hamlet's dying wish is for Fortinbras to claim the Danish throne.
- **[[Claudius]]:** He neutralizes Fortinbras's initial threat through shrewd diplomacy with Old Norway. This highlights his charisma and ability to maneuver in politics.

### Pivotal Moments
- **The [[First Court Scene]] (Act 1, Scene 2):** Fortinbras is first introduced off-stage as an imminent military threat
- **[[Fortinbras' troops]] (Act 4, Scene 4):** Crosses Denmark with his army to fight over a worthless patch of Polish land, triggering Hamlet's "How all occasions do inform against me" soliloquy and commitment to act
- **[[The Duel]] (Act 5, Scene 2):** He arrives at Elsinore promptly after the royal family wipes themselves out to claim the throne and restore order in a power vacuum

### Important Quotes
- *"Witness this army of such mass and charge / Led by a delicate and tender prince..."* (Act 4, Scene 4): Hamlet marvels at the sight of Fortinbras' army (and the twenty thousand lives put at risk for a small piece of land)
- *"For me, with sorrow I embrace my fortune. / I have some rights of memory in this kingdom...* (Act 5, Scene 2): Fortinbras accepts the Danish crown (while probably also processing all the corpses of the royal line)
