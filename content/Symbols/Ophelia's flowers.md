### Layers of Meaning
- **Disguised Protest**: Ophelia is stripped of agency by society and subject to the control of her father, brother, and lover. As a result, she can't speak up for herself in a traditional sense. After going mad, she uses floral symbolism to publicly indict the court's hypocrisy, [[corruption]], and guilt. She finally gets to speak truths to the faces of all the guilty members of court, nothing any sane courtier would survive speaking.
- **Shattered Innocence & Betrayal:** Specific (imaginary) herbs and flowers she hands out to people represent different emotional wounds inflicted on her. There are broken promises (of love?), unremembered [[loyalty]], unjust and unnatural death.
- **Self-Elegy:** Ophelia gathers and adorns herself with wildflowers before she drowns. In a way, this probably changes how she is remembered. It's powerful and memorable imagery, and self-memorializing. The flowers might've saved her from being lost in the past as some passive court pawn.

### Key Appearances & Analysis
**[[Ophelia's descent]]** in Act 4, Scene 5 is where she distributes imaginary flowers to [[Laertes]], 
[[Claudius]], and [[Gertrude]]. It's also the first time we see her after the [[death of Polonius]]. Below are some notable flowers, who they go to, and what they represent:
- *Rosemary* (remembrance) and *Pansies* (thoughts) for Laertes, urging him to remember their murdered father.
- *Fennel* (flattery/deceit) and *Columbines* (ingratitude/infidelity) for Claudius (and Gertrude), to call out their corrupt marriage and phony behavior ([[performance]]).
- *Rue* (sorrow and repentance) Ophelia shares with Gertrude, whom she tells to wear "with a difference", meaning the purpose of the herb varies by person. For Gertrude, it may mean repentance for her lack of grief over Old King Hamlet and hasty marriage to Claudius. For herself, it represents personal [[grief]] over the death of her father (and the betrayal by Hamlet).
- *Daisies* (innocence/purity) she references but doesn't give out, implying that innocence has been lost and/or corrupted.
- *Violets* (faithfulness/fidelity) Ophelia notes all withered when her father died, signifying the end of loyalty and virtue (in court?).
Later, Gertrude reports Ophelia's death (Act 4, Scene 7). She recalls that Ophelia weaved garlands of crowflowers, nettles, daisies, and other flowers before falling into the brook.

### Related Characters & Themes
##### Characters
- [[Ophelia]] exercises a fragmented voice after a life of being shrunken and shriveled by the men around her
- [[Gertrude]] gets roasted the hardest (moral rebuke) via the rue and tells the story of the death of Ophelia
- [[Claudius]] is exposed for deception via fennel
- [[Laertes]] is pushed further towards vengeance by Ophelia's symbolic please for remembrance
##### Themes & Motifs
- [[Madness]] is (tragic irony) what enables Ophelia to finally speak for herself. No one can persecute or punish a victim of psychological collapse.
- [[Grief]] over a dead father, sound familiar? But seriously, here we have more unprocessed mourning turning inward.
- [[Loyalty]] is gone, fidelity is dead, and it's all represented by withered violets.
- [[Isolation]] is what makes the loss and injustice so brutal for everyone, and here for Ophelia. Besides, the men playing tug-of-war with her and squashing her individuality.
