The past has yet to pass, and it demands restitution. But is this divine justice or demonic deception? Huh, strange how your own father is threatening damnation unless you shed blood.

### Layers of Meaning
- **Unresolved Past:** The Ghost embodies historical trauma and secret crime refusing to stay buried. Until Old King Hamlet's murder is answered/avenged, Denmark will remain politically and spiritually paralyzed ("time is out of joint").
- **Ambiguity:** This thing could be a soul from purgatory, but what about a demon exploiting   [[grief]]? If this ghost turns out to be malevolent, Hamlet could have fallen into a trap of eternal damnation. This uncertainty is an engine of Hamlet's delay.
- **Revenge Ethics:** It appears in full battle armor. Perhaps it's a clash of ethics. Warrior code demands vengeance, Hamlet is a human with a conscience. Blood vengeance may permanently scar your mind.

### Key Appearances & Analysis
- **The Battlements (Act 1, Scenes 1&4):** It appears at midnight in the same armor worn in battle against Old Fortinbras. The Ghost signals to [[Horatio]] and the other guards that Denmark faces impending military and moral crisis ("something is rotten").
- **The [[Ghost's Appearance]] (Act 1, Scene 5):** The Ghost gets Hamlet alone and reveals Claudius's regicide/fratricide (*see* [[corruption]]). It then commands Hamlet to "Revenge his foul and most unnatural murder", but also to spare Gertrude. This sparks Hamlet's antic disposition (feigned [[madness]]).
- **Right before the [[death of Polonius]] (Act 3, Scene 4):** It reappears in Gertrude's room/closet, but is only visible to Hamlet. The Ghost is there to "whet Hamlet's almost blunted purpose" (remind Hamlet of his task at hand?) and protect Gertrude from Hamlet's verbal attacks (*see* [[misogyny]]). Since Gertrude doesn't see anything, this final appearance suggests this may not be an objective supernatural reality. Maybe Hamlet really is just schizo.

### Related Characters & Themes
##### Characters
- [[Hamlet]] is bound by duty as a son to obey a command despite not being able to verify its nature
- [[Claudius]] is the target of the accusation and revenge plot
- [[Gertrude]] does not perceive the ghost in Act 3, so either Hamlet was hallucinating or maybe she is spiritually blind
- [[Horatio]] was a skeptic until he saw it, at which point he validates that the Ghost is not merely Hamlet's imagination (at least not in Act 1)
##### Themes & Motifs
- [[Certainty & Truth]] because this begs a question: is the ghost an honest spirit or a lying devil?
- [[Revenge vs Justice]] is a tension for Hamlet, after the ghost imposes an impossible, contradictory moral command on him.
- [[Action vs Inaction]] is what paradoxically freezes Hamlet after he is unable to determine the right course of action, or even what he can believe in all this.
- [[Appearance vs Reality]] becomes a question since the ghost was only visible to everyone in Act 1, and its reappearance in Act 3 was only visible to Hamlet. Perhaps there was never a reappearance and Hamlet was just hallucinating out of the guilt of putting off revenge.
