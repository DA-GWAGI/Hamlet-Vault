Thing that wiped out the entire royal family. But also a metaphor for the infection of guilt, deceit, and rumors within a political state.

### Layers of Meaning
- **Literal Murder Weapon:** It's literally the thing used to kill everyone, the agent of political assassination if you will. First, [[Claudius]] pours poison into Old King Hamlet's ear, and later in Act 5 everyone dies from poisoned blades and/or goblets containing poisoned wine.
- **Political & Moral [[Corruption]]:** Also a metaphor for how illegitimate [[power]], hypocrisy, and rumors will infect a state from the top down. Claudius's crime is rotting the court's moral integrity, though there is no visible/immediate external wound.
- **Figurative Language:** Old King Hamlet dies by having poison poured in his ear. What else is poison to the ear? Manipulation and lies. Claudius is probably the biggest perpetrator, and the narrative he weaves about his brother's death poisons the public's perception of reality. Also, both [[Polonius]] and Claudius constantly spy and meddle ([[eavesdropping]]) in other people's conversations, trapping them in their "poisonous" schemes.

### Key Appearances & Analysis
- **The [[Ghost's Appearance]] (Act 1, Scene 5):** [[The ghost]] describes being poisoned and how it's a physical horror, going through your body and crusting up your smooth skin from the inside out. This is powerful imagery for the rest of the play: internal treachery produces outward rot.
- **The [[Mousetrap]] (Act 3, Scene 2):** [[Hamlet]] stages a player pouring poison into the player King's ear. Re-enacting this event poisons Claudius psychologically, who then admits his hidden guilt, first in an [[aside]] and then in a long [[soliloquy]].
- **[[The Duel]] (Act 5, Scene 2):** Poison is multiplied here. [[Laertes]]'s poisoned sword tip, Claudius's poisoned wine, everything just kind of feels poisoned. Their scheme backfires, and Gertrude, Laertes, Hamlet, and Claudius (the perpetrator) all end up dead. This proves that deceitful violence inevitably poisons and consumes its own architects.

### Related Characters & Themes
##### Characters
- [[Claudius]] is the primary poisoner, his crimes are covert and his killings are non-confrontational
- [[Laertes]] was an honorable avenger (albeit rash and impulsive) who gets corrupted into treacherous poisoner by Claudius
- [[Gertrude]] was an accidental victim who drinks the poison (ironically, after the ghost specifically says to spare her in Act 1)
##### Themes & Motifs
- [[Corruption]] is the literalization of "something is rotten in Denmark"
- [[Appearance vs Reality]] is the disparity between a healthy-appearing exterior and the unseen poison working beneath
- [[Power]] (usurped authority) is obtained (and sustained) through covert murder
- [[Revenge vs Justice]], since in Hamlet's attempt to avenge his father, he dies a very similar death (via poison from \[the big\] C~~laudius~~)
