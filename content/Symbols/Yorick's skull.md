Some may have wit, beauty, royal status. But when all is said and done, kings and peasants decompose into dust the same.

### Layers of Meaning
- **The Absolute Equality of Death:** Human remains (in this case Yorick's skull) strip away every social/earthly distinction. Wit, beauty, political rank, royal blood, [[power]], all else as well. When you die you get put in dirt, where a court jester becomes indistinguishable from Julius Caesar.
- **Futility of Ambition & Scheming:** [[Hamlet]] holds the skull right before [[the duel]] with [[Laertes]]. He realizes the permanence of biological decay renders human plotting completely absurd. This finally cures Hamlet's obsessive need to engineer cosmic justice and find an absolute truth (*see* [[certainty & truth]]), ending his psychological arc.
- **Realism:** Everyone in Elsinore seems to be obsessed with appearance and [[performance]]. Here, Hamlet uses bare bones to mock the courtly vanity (and also female cosmetics, perhaps with a hint of [[misogyny]]). He states that no amount of surface performance can disguise the skull underneath.

### Key Appearances & Analysis
**The [[Gravedigger Scene]] (Act 5, Scene 1):** Two gravediggers are digging Ophelia's grave when one of them tosses up Yorick's skull, which they identify. Hamlet comes back (from his kidnapping by pirates) and picks up the skull, shifting from abstract (and romanticized) meditations on death ("[[To be, or not to be]]") to visceral physical disgust ("my gorge rises at it"). 

He banters with the gravediggers, who surprisingly hold their own and are able to match his wordplay (*see* [[puns]]) and verbal gymnastics. Throughout the play, the gravediggers may be the only characters capable of matching Hamlet in this, reinforcing the idea of absurdity around class divide and rank. Despite their commoner upbringing (and likely meager education), they are clearly intellectually on par with Hamlet.

### Related Characters & Themes
##### Characters
- [[Hamlet]] experiences his final psychological pivot (toward accepting fate) after picking up the skull
- [[Horatio]] is there as a grounded realist to keep Hamlet's morbid imagination in check
- [[Ophelia]]'s freshly dug grave is the site where the skull is found
##### Themes & Motifs
- [[Death & Mortality]] is the central physical emblem of human transience
- [[Existentialism]] is a sort of tension in Hamlet's mind that he resolves by holding the skull, as he is forced to confront the lack of inherent (cosmic) permanence
- [[Appearance vs Reality]] goes strong here, Hamlet is seeing the bare skull (reality) beneath all the painted faces and royal masks (appearance)
